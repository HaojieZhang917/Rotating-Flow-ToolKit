Effects of surface mass flux on the stability and receptivity of the rotor
boundary layer in a rotor–stator system
         Haojie Zhang1, 2 and Jianxin Liu1, 2
         1) Department of Mechanics, Tianjin University, Tianjin 300072, China
         2) National Key Laboratory of Vehicle Power System, Tianjin 300072, China

         (*Corresponding author: shookware@tju.edu.cn)
         (Dated: 13 September 2026)
         ABSTRACT
         Rotor–stator configurations are common in turbomachinery, where rotating boundary layers may undergo instability
         and transition. A local linear framework is developed to determine how surface mass flux modifies the stability and
         receptivity of the rotor boundary layer in a rotor–stator cavity. The rotating core provides an outer flow condition that
         differs from that over an isolated rotating disk. The base flow, convective and absolute instabilities, and receptivity are
         analyzed under injection, zero surface mass flux, and suction. Injection thickens the rotor boundary layer, expands the
         unstable domain of the inviscid Type I mode, and increases its growth rate. At representative conditions, the growth rate
         of the viscous Type II mode is less sensitive to surface mass flux than that of the Type I mode. Contributions from the
         base flow and its gradients largely cancel for the Type II mode. The representative Type I adjoint mode is concentrated
         near the critical layer. The principal Type II peak occurs where the effective shear vanishes. A radially localized
         wall displacement with a specified azimuthal mode number represents stationary roughness or unsteady wall forcing.
         Combining local receptivity with spatial growth gives the downstream disturbance level. The radius of maximum local
         receptivity does not necessarily produce the largest downstream amplitude because the available growth interval also
         matters.
         Keywords: Linear stability, rotor boundary layer, surface mass flux, rotor–stator system, adjoint receptivity
